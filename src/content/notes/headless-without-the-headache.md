I've rebuilt everything from a [headless Shopify store](/works/hummingbird-hammocks) to national organisation sites on headless Craft CMS. The stack changed every time. The thing that decided whether the project felt easy or painful never did: **the content model**.

## Model content, not pages

Pages change; content types endure. When we modelled *things* (products, events, stories, locations) instead of today's page layouts, marketing teams could publish and edit on their own, and redesigns stopped requiring a content migration.

## Editors are users too

A headless build that developers love and editors hate is a failure with good Lighthouse scores. On the sites I worked on at Hungry Hippo, setting up models so teams could publish independently is what actually moved the needle. User engagement rose 60% across those sites, because content stayed fresh.

## Performance is a feature editors feel

Headless Gatsby and Next.js front ends made sites dramatically faster. The Hummingbird Hammocks rebuild loaded quicker, grew traffic and cut bounce. Speed isn't vanity; it's the difference between a store that sells and one that leaks visitors.

## Pick boring delivery, spend novelty on content

GraphQL or REST, Gatsby or Next.js, Craft or Sanity, all of them work. Spend your risk budget on modelling and editor experience, keep delivery predictable, and automate publishing so nobody waits on a developer to press a button.

That philosophy is also why this very site is now built with Astro and Markdown, the simplest pipeline that keeps content portable.
